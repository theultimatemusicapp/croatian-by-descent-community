## Start with the life you want to lead

Write down what an ordinary week would need to include: work, school if relevant, healthcare, errands, time outdoors and social contact. Use that list to compare places. A town that is right for a summer visit may ask different things of you during the rest of the year.

## Separate permission to stay from the practical move

Before paying deposits or booking a one-way journey, identify which authority can confirm your right to live and work in Croatia. Citizenship, residence and permission to work are different questions. Ask the relevant authority how your nationality and circumstances affect your options; do not assume a relative’s experience applies to you.

## Test a realistic budget

List rent, utilities, food, transport, insurance, document costs and travel back to your current home. Collect dated quotations and distinguish one-off expenses from monthly costs. Build a reserve for delays and unexpected repairs instead of relying on a single internet estimate.

## Look at housing in person

Check the duration of the tenancy, what is included, who is authorised to sign and how repairs are handled. Ask about heating, cooling, damp, noise, internet and what happens when the tourist season changes. Record the condition of the property before moving in.

## Plan your first month

Create a checklist for the arrangements that apply to you: official registration, healthcare, banking, utilities and connectivity. Keep documents organised, allow time for appointments and verify current instructions directly with the relevant service.

## Give community the same attention as paperwork

Choose one recurring activity that you would enjoy even if you did not meet anyone immediately. Language practice, volunteering, sport and interest groups offer reasons to see the same people again. A connection to Croatia can be meaningful while settling in still takes time.

## Before you decide

Consider a trial stay that reflects your intended daily routine. Test the commute, shop for ordinary groceries and spend time away from the main visitor streets. Ask residents what is difficult as well as what they love.
